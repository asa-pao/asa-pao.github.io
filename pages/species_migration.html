Seasonal Behaviors of White-Tailed Ptarmigan

Mapping potential migration patterns of White-Tailed Ptarmigan populations with GBIF.

Species: Lagopus Leucura (White-Tailed Ptarmigan) GBIF Dataset: https://doi.org/10.15468/dl.e47j6v Photo by Author
Research question

What do the observations illustrate over time?

This project begins with the question: What are the seasonal behaviors of White-Tailed Ptarmigan populations, and how can they be mapped using GBIF data?

The White-Tailed Ptarmigan is a high-altitude species, often found in alpine and subalpine environments. Understanding potential migration patterns could be crucial for conservation efforts, especially in the context of climate change. This project aims to map these behaviors using GBIF data from 2024.

About the Data

GBIF Data

This study pulls observations for White-Tailed Ptarmigan from the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF). This project speifically looks at all data from January 1, 2024 through December 31, 2024. It should be noted that this is simply the request window, not the extent of all data (not all days contain observations).

The data was then processed, aggregated to ecoregions, and normalized for analysis. Observe the brief overview of the data used below:

Results

Findings + Discussion

This said, there are some initial limitations. Lagopus Leucura (White-Tailed Ptarmigan) are high-altitude species, and their observations may be sparse and unevenly distributed across the landscape. Additionally, GBIF data may have gaps or inaccuracies, which could affect the mapping of seasonal behaviors. Further, these species are not migratory, so no noticeable change should be observed.

As the map shows, the distribution of White-Tailed Ptarmigan observations changes over time, with certain ecoregions experiencing more frequent sightings in different months. Yet, there is no clear sign of migration or any spatial pattern. This emphasizes the importance of considering temporal factors when interpreting species occurrence data. This said, a lot of the ecoregions highlighted throughout 2024 are consistent with data from eBird (Cornell Lab), Birds of the World, and other reputable sources.

Limitations

Uncertainty + Questions + Next Steps

There are many limitations to this exploratory project. Some of them include:

  • Coverage: missing data + accuracy of observations
  • Nature: White-Tailed Ptarmigan are not migratory
  • Seasonality: alpine observations are influenced by seasonal changes

Given these limitations, any conclusions drawn from the map should be interpreted with caution. Future work could involve collecting more comprehensive data, improving observation accuracy, and incorporating additional environmental variables to better understand the factors influencing White-Tailed Ptarmigan distribution over time or even the frequency of observations.
