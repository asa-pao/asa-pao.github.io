Mt. Rainier, Washington

Examining Changes in Temperature at Paradise Station, Mt. Rainier

A exploratory analysis of long-term temperature observations from the NOAA Paradise station on Mount Rainier.

Mt. Rainier (MRNP) NOAA Station ID: USC00456898
Research question

What do the observations illustrate over time?

This project begins with the question: how have recorded temperatures (TOBS or Temperature at the Time of Observation) at the Paradise weather station varied over its available observations? Does it suggest a warming trend?

The paradise sits on Mount Rainier’s southern slopes, just below the summit at 1650 meters and within a steep, mountanous environment. See map below for spatial context. I hypothesize that due to global increasing temperatures, data will show a slight increase in temperature. MAP GOES HERE ASA

However, one must note the difference in elevation between the summit of Mt. Rainier and the paradise station (Mt. Rainier Summit: 4,392m, Paradise Station: 1650m). The difference matters because within 2742 meters, local conditions can drastically change. In other words, temperatures in this study reflect solely Paradise Station, not temperatures across the whole mountain. As such, no inference can be made on how the mountain as a whole is experiencing a changing climate, although recent LiDAR suggests a change (https://pugetsoundlidar.ess.washington.edu/lidardata/restricted/filegeodatabase/mtrainierNP/index.html). <br> It cannot by itself describe the full elevation gradient, the differences between slopes, or conditions at the summit. Those questions would require additional stations, gridded climate products, or a separate elevation-based analysis.

About the Data

Preparing: NOAA + Daily Temperature Records

This study pulls daily observations for stationUSC00456898from NOAA’s National Centers for Environmental Information (NCEI), using the Daily Summaries service (API). This project speifically looks at all data from January 1, 1917 through January 1, 2026. It should be noted that this is simply the request window, not the extent of all data (not all days contain observations).

The data was then filtered to the field TOBS and converted to Celcius (tempC). Before graphing, the series was resampled into annual means (this helps with the inconsistencies within the TOBS sampling). Observe the brief overview of the data used below:
